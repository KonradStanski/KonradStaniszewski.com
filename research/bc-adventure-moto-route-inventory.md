# British Columbia adventure-moto route inventory

**Research cutoff:** 2026-09-07  
**Geographic emphasis:** Lower Mainland, Vancouver Island, Canadian Rockies, Kootenays, and the southern/central Interior  
**Use:** research basis for a future full-screen route-discovery map—not a turn-by-turn guide or a statement that any route is currently legal or passable

## Executive conclusion

There is not yet a released official British Columbia Backcountry Discovery Route. BDR says its British Columbia route is being scouted and is expected to be the first Canadian Discovery Route to launch “in the coming years.” The closest things available now are:

1. **Adventure Routes Canada (ARC):** the newest national adventure-motorcycle map and GPX collection, including British Columbia.
2. **Trans Canada Adventure Trail (TCAT):** the historic long-distance backbone across mainland BC and Vancouver Island.
3. **Vancouver Island Grand Loop (VIGL):** the clearest published Island-scale itinerary: about 1,700 km in 18 stages.
4. **North Island 1000:** the best concentrated north-Island BDR-style loop, with more than 1,000 km of remote gravel.
5. **EVADRS:** the strongest local, moto-specific route bundle for the Elk Valley and East Kootenays.
6. **Forestry Trunk Road (Alberta):** the nearest established Rockies-scale analogue to a BDR, with more than 1,000 km from the Crowsnest area toward Grande Prairie.

The research sweep evaluated **69 route, corridor, spur, and riding-area records**. That number deliberately includes fragments and historical leads. Several famous lines are currently broken, restricted, or too uncertain to present as through routes—most notably the Fernie–Flathead loop, the Kettle Valley Rail Trail as an end-to-end moto route, and parts of the Squamish/Elaho system.

The map should therefore be a **curated discovery and evidence product**, not a collection of copied GPX files. Every route needs a source, verification date, confidence grade, access state, vehicle class, and track-reuse state.

## How to read the inventory

| Map treatment | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Primary** | Strong published route or named corridor; still requires a same-day access check. |
| **Candidate** | Credible route or road chain, but current continuity, legality, or condition needs validation. |
| **Spur** | Worthwhile out-and-back or access road; do not imply a through connection. |
| **Trail system** | Designated technical riding area rather than BDR-style travel. Keep it in a distinct layer. |
| **Historical / constraint** | Useful research lead, closure, or non-motorized boundary. Do not publish it as a rideable route. |

“High confidence” means the route identity or restriction is well supported. It never guarantees that the road is open today. Resource roads are industrial roads; washouts, active hauling, wildfire, gates, snow, wildlife closures, and field signage can supersede this document at any time.

### Lower Mainland hard exclusions

- The **Stawamus / Indian River / Indian Arm through line** was closed to all users at the km 3 gate through 2026-12-31; the Province explicitly says there is no through access to Vancouver/Indian Arm.
- Do not draw any continuation beyond the **Squamish River Mud Creek closure** until the Province posts a repaired/open state.
- Wildlife motor-vehicle prohibitions on Ashlu, Elaho, Callaghan, Lillooet South, and other Sea-to-Sky roads are rules, not soft advisories.
- Do not present the **Harrison East–Kookipi–Nahatlatch** corridor as an emergency highway bypass.
- Do not derive Sunshine Coast through-connections from base-map road linework alone.

### Vancouver Island hard exclusions and rules

- Mosaic’s [access page](https://www.mosaicforests.com/access) is the controlling weekly source for its private managed forest land. Outside designated places/times, access by any means is trespass. ATV access is club-agreement-only; because the FAQ does not clearly address plated dual-sports, the map must not infer permission.
- **Mount Sicker** has been explicitly closed to public access since 2026-04-14.
- Historical Donner/Kunlin GPX lines that enter **Strathcona Provincial Park** are not motorcycle routes. In provincial parks, motorized travel is limited to roads or specifically authorized/signed areas.
- BC Hydro’s **Strathcona Dam road** work is a multi-year interruption beginning in late 2025. Older loop files need a reroute.
- Do not turn bicycle-only trail, hiking trail, or water-taxi geometry into a motorcycle route merely because part of a research overlay follows FSRs.

### Interior and Rockies hard exclusions

- The **Kettle Valley / Columbia & Western rail-trail system is not one motorcycle-legal route**. Rules differ by segment. Some are explicitly non-motorized or prohibit highway vehicles. The 67 km Princeton–Coquihalla section has been closed since 2021; a severely damaged 17 km portion is being decommissioned beginning in 2026. Use the system as a legal-status/constraint layer, not an ADV line. See the [2026 provincial notice](https://archive.news.gov.bc.ca/releases/news_releases_2024-2028/2026ENV0029-000723.htm) and [RDOS access-restriction map](https://www.rdos.bc.ca/assets/COMMS-2025/Kettle-Valley-Rail-Trail-Track-Access-Restrictions-Map-V6.pdf).
- The **Great Divide Mountain Bike Route is not motorcycle-legal end to end**. It includes singletrack and non-motorized park sections. In Banff and Jasper, motorcycles remain on legal roads; no off-road dirt-bike route should be inferred through the parks. See [Banff rules](https://www.parks.canada.ca/pn-np/ab/banff/securite-safety/regles-rules) and [Jasper rules](https://parks.canada.ca/pn-np/ab/jasper/securite-safety/regles-rules).
- The historic **Fernie–Flathead loop** is broken and should be displayed only as fragments plus closure markers.
- Gray Creek Pass’s scheduled repair-end time is not proof that the work completed. Require a same-day official or local confirmation before setting `open`.
- Alberta Public Land Use Zones generally confine motorized use to designated roads/trails. Highway 734 treatment changes by segment; encode the Shared Use Road layer rather than a blanket permission.

## Current access and legal baseline

1. **BC Forest Service Roads:** a motorcycle on an FSR must comply with the Motor Vehicle Act framework, including registration/insurance requirements; the Forest Service Road Use Regulation requires at least CAD $200,000 third-party liability and caps speed at the lower of 80 km/h or the posted limit. See the [current regulation](https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/15_70_2004).
2. **BC Crown-land ORVs:** off-road vehicles on Crown land must be registered or licensed under the applicable framework. See the [Off-Road Vehicle Act](https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/14005).
3. **Industrial-road reality:** roads may close temporarily, hazards may be unsigned, and unused roads may be deactivated. Start from the [BC resource-road safety hub](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/industry/natural-resource-use/resource-roads/local-road-safety-information) and obey posted radio/signage instructions. The [resource-road radio guidance](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/industry/natural-resource-use/resource-roads/radio-communications) explains posted channels.
4. **Provincial parks:** motorized use is limited to legal roads or specifically authorized/signed areas. A park trail beside an access road is not a continuation of the moto route.
5. **Private managed forest land:** landowner permission and current gates control; a road visible in OSM or a GPX is not evidence of access.
6. **Wildfire:** always check the [BC Wildfire map](https://wildfiresituation.nrs.gov.bc.ca/map?featureType=British_Columbia_Bans_and_Prohibition_Areas), evacuation orders, and landowner notices. A regional fire-centre restriction may lift while a local closure remains.
7. **Highway/current event layer:** [Open511](https://api.open511.gov.bc.ca/events?format=kml&status=ACTIVE) can supply active BC transportation events, but it does not cover every FSR or private road.
8. **Alberta public land:** check the [official recreation maps](https://www.alberta.ca/public-land-recreation-maps), [PLUZ regulations](https://www.alberta.ca/public-land-use-zones-regulations), and [current closures](https://www.alberta.ca/public-land-closures) before publishing a Rockies status.

## Map data and rights strategy

### Geometry policy

Use three geometry classes:

- **Open geometry:** OSM/ODbL road data, appropriately licensed BC government open data, or geometry authored from independently verified public facts.
- **Permissioned geometry:** a publisher has explicitly licensed the map to host its GPX. Retain licence, attribution, version, and checksum.
- **Link-only:** ARC, TCAT, VIGL, North Island 1000, EVADRS, Backroad Status, Trailforks, commercial sources, or any source without a clear republication licence. Show a route card, broad corridor/region, and a link to the owner; do not trace or embed their track.

The app should never reconstruct a protected GPX by visually tracing it over base-map roads. Public facts such as route name, endpoints, general character, and source link can seed an independently verified corridor, but the final geometry needs its own lawful provenance.

### Implemented geometry-resolution pass (2026-09-09)

The local GPX set now contains two explicitly labelled geometry qualities:

- **OSM road-following overview:** 16 routes were independently road-matched against OpenStreetMap and accepted only when every control point snapped within 2 km and the routed distance remained within a conservative published-distance envelope. Ten point-to-point tracks use ordered via-point routing; six closed circuits use loop-specific stop optimization. Together they contain 72,060 vertices.
- **Coarse corridor:** 53 routes remain broad research lines because automatic road matching failed the snap/distance checks, the record represents an area or closure, or car routing could materially misrepresent a technical trail. These routes contain 226 vertices and are labelled as coarse in the UI and GPX metadata.

The complete per-route accept/reject evidence is stored in [`bc-backroads-geometry-audit.json`](./bc-backroads-geometry-audit.json). Rendered QA exposed unreliable topology when closed loops were sent through ordered point-to-point routing; the Vancouver Island Grand Loop, Pacific Marine Circle, North Island 1000, Tugwell–Alligator, Youbou–Port Alberni–Bamfield, and Lac du Bois circuit were restored and then rematched with closed-circuit stop optimization. Original control points are retained for accepted matches so an OSM match can be reproduced or replaced. OSM-derived GPXs include contributor and ODbL attribution; no publisher GPX was copied into the repository.

### Minimum route record

```ts
type RouteRecord = {
  id: string
  name: string
  region: string[]
  kind: 'route' | 'corridor' | 'spur' | 'trail-system' | 'constraint'
  status: 'open-reported' | 'verify' | 'seasonal' | 'closed' | 'historical'
  statusCheckedAt: string
  statusSourceUrls: string[]
  evidenceGrade: 'A' | 'B' | 'C'
  vehicleClass: ('big-adv' | 'dual-sport' | 'light-dual-sport' | 'ohv')[]
  season?: string
  distanceKm?: number
  geometryPolicy: 'open' | 'permissioned' | 'link-only'
  geometrySource?: string
  routeOwnerUrl?: string
  warnings: string[]
}
```

Grade A should mean an organizer/land-manager source plus a current access source; B means a credible published route with incomplete current validation; C means a historical or community lead. A route can be Grade A evidence and still have `status: 'closed'`.

### Dynamic overlays worth integrating

- BC government recreation sites/trails and alert layers.
- Natural-resource district closures and deactivations; where no API exists, store reviewed notices with timestamps rather than scraping a false real-time status.
- BC Parks boundaries/alerts, DriveBC/Open511 events, wildfire bans and incidents, evacuation areas, and motor-vehicle prohibition polygons.
- Private-land/access warnings, especially Mosaic, as links and boundary notices—not assumptions derived from gate symbols.
- Alberta PLUZ/designated-road layers and closures for the Rockies view.

## Preliminary map stack recommendation

For the eventual build, use **MapLibre GL JS**. It is the leading open-source WebGL vector-map library, fits a full-screen explorer, supports vector styling and terrain, and keeps the tile provider replaceable.

For the first release:

- Use [OpenFreeMap](https://openfreemap.org/) as the default public vector style/tile source. It requires no API key and is intended as a free public map service.
- Keep a provider interface so the deployment is not coupled to a donation-funded endpoint.
- Do **not** use the public `tile.openstreetmap.org` raster service as a production tile backend. Its [tile usage policy](https://operations.osmfoundation.org/policies/tiles/) provides no SLA and prohibits bulk/offline use.
- If usage grows or offline regional downloads become a requirement, self-host a BC/Alberta extract as [PMTiles](https://docs.protomaps.com/pmtiles/maplibre) behind object storage/CDN. This gives predictable costs and HTTP-range delivery while keeping MapLibre.
- Preserve OpenStreetMap attribution and ODbL compliance, and attribute every route/overlay separately.

In a Next.js project, MapLibre should be loaded client-side. Current MapLibre documentation also needs to be checked when implementation starts because worker bundling details change between major versions.

## Recommended first map release

The safest, most useful initial release is not “every GPX on one map.” It is:

1. Full-screen MapLibre map centered on southern BC with region filters.
2. Cards and broad independently sourced corridors for the six primary frameworks: ARC (link-only), TCAT (link-only), VIGL (link-only), North Island 1000 (link-only), EVADRS (link-only), and Alberta FTR (link-only until track rights are clear).
3. Independently built/open geometry for a small set of validated public-road modules: Harrison–Pemberton, Hurley/Gold Bridge, Tree to Sea Drive, Highway 31, Koocanusa designated network, and selected official OHV systems.
4. Prominent `last checked`, `source`, `status confidence`, `bike class`, `season`, and `not a navigation guarantee` fields.
5. Closure/constraint markers for Gray Creek verification, Squamish River/Ashlu, Fernie–Flathead, KVR fragments, Mount Sicker, Mosaic access, park motorized boundaries, and seasonal wildlife closures.

Before publishing precise geometry, obtain written reuse answers from ARC, VIGL, North Island 1000, TCAT/Gravel Travel, EVADRS, and Backroad Status. That one rights pass will determine how much of version one is a route map versus a sourced discovery index.

## Research limitations

- This is desk research, not a field scout. It intentionally distinguishes route identity from current access.
- No source covers all FSRs. Provincial notices, crowd reports, landowner gates, and field signs can disagree or change within hours.
- Motorcycle legality was not inferred from bicycle routes; bicycle sources appear only where they provide road-condition or corridor leads.
- Distances and difficulty labels use publisher estimates and are not normalized across sources.
- Indigenous reserve closures, private parcels, wildlife areas, and park boundaries still need geometry-level reconciliation before drawing any exact through line.
- No publisher GPX was copied into this repository. The higher-resolution local tracks are independently road-matched from OpenStreetMap data; exact publisher tracks remain link-only unless redistribution permission is obtained.

## Research decision

Proceed to mapping, but treat it as an **evidence-backed route discovery map with explicit uncertainty**, not a navigation product. The research supports enough high-quality material for a compelling Lower Mainland/Island/Rockies launch, while the closure and licensing findings argue strongly against publishing an unqualified pile of tracks.
